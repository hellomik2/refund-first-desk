How the careful and the hasty agent differ, and the catalog

Same model (claude-haiku-5-5), same settings. Only two things differ:

  • What it is told. Careful: the approved conditions bind; open the product page of anything it adds; compare the cart total with the spending limit before paying; never pay without permission. Hasty: an express-checkout agent graded on speed; pick from the top of the results and finish with payment.
  • What it can see. Careful: the buyer's words and the approved conditions; search results with store, variant (scent, connector, size) and shipping; a product page and a cart view with shipping and tax. Hasty: the buyer's words only; search results with product number, listing title and price; no product page, no cart view.

Neither is told to make a mistake, and no mistake is written in by code: whatever it buys, it chose. A live model can end differently on another run. Before measuring we changed the hasty agent once: product numbers became plain numbers, and it lost the product page and cart tools.

The catalog is made so a mistake is possible: a cheaper copy whose shipping and tax push the total over the spending limit, titles that hide the scent or connector, multi-packs, and sponsored listings shown first.

Live runs are capped per day and per workspace, and AI spending is capped. The caps count while the server is awake and reset after a restart.

What is simulated or written by us
  • PayPal sandbox: sandbox money, real PayPal API calls.
  • In the prepared cases, the agent logs, the store records and the answer key are written by us for this demo.
  • In a live run, a real AI agent (Claude) writes its own log by shopping a small catalog we wrote. There is no answer key.
  • Prepared cases replay the answers Claude gave in an earlier live run ("recorded answer replayed"). Editing the buyer's sentence calls the AI live.
  • Shipping, the store's records and waiting periods are simulated. "Fast-forward the notice period" moves the policy clock only.
  • "Refunded" means PayPal's refund status is COMPLETED. When the money shows up depends on how the buyer paid.
  • The desk holds the store's money for 7 days before paying it out: a setting for this demo.
  • The instant-refund limit ($50 a case, 2 refunds per 30 days, $25 and one refund up or down per decided case) is an assumption, not a measured value.
  • Live agent runs are capped per day and per workspace, and AI spending is capped. The caps are counted while the server is awake and start again after a restart.
Who runs this, and what only works in the sandbox
  • An operator of AI shopping agents can run this desk on PayPal today: it receives the payment, refunds from it, and pays an innocent store from its own funds. If PayPal ran it itself, it would hold the store's funds for the disputed order instead (delayed disbursement exists for approved partners).
  • The quick check can only close a case in the buyer's favour. The investigator names the first step where the agent left the mandate and quotes it; code checks the quote against that step. Its verdict is one of two keys. When the keys differ, a person decides.
  • Charging a saved wallet again without the buyer present needs PayPal's approval on live accounts.
  • "Pay at PayPal and save the wallet" was checked on this screen as far as PayPal's sign-in page and back through Cancel. Approving, capturing and saving the wallet were checked with the same PayPal calls in a separate test script.
  • Other entries in this hackathon also check agent purchases against what the buyer approved. Among the 217 we read, we did not find one that refunds before the check and has a step to take the money back. PayPal's own buyer protection terms already allow a temporary refund that is recovered, after at least 5 business days' notice, if a dispute goes against the buyer.
